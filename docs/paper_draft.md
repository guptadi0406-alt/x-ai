# Replication and Extension of "A Perspective on Explainable AI Methods: SHAP and LIME"

## Abstract

We replicate and extend the work of Salih et al. (2024) which investigates how machine-learning model choice and feature collinearity affect the reliability of SHAP and LIME explanations. Using the Airline Passenger Satisfaction dataset, we reproduce the paper's case study with four classifiers (Decision Tree, Logistic Regression, LightGBM, and Support Vector Classifier), demonstrating that feature importance rankings are indeed model-dependent and affected by collinearity. We reimplemented the paper's proposed NMR (Normalized Movement Rate) and MIP (Modified Index Position) metrics to assess ranking stability and adjust for collinearity. As an extension, we propose a Correlation-Aware XAI Robustness Framework that combines hierarchical feature grouping, group-level importance aggregation, and multi-seed stability analysis to provide more robust and interpretable explanations. Our extension is evaluated against baseline SHAP and MIP-adjusted rankings using Spearman rank correlation, Kendall's tau, and Jaccard similarity metrics.

**Keywords:** Explainable AI, SHAP, LIME, Feature Importance, Collinearity, Model Dependency, Ranking Stability

## 1. Introduction

Machine learning models have achieved remarkable performance across domains, but their complexity raises concerns about transparency and interpretability (Salih et al., 2024). Explainable AI (XAI) methods such as SHAP (Lundberg & Lee, 2017) and LIME (Ribeiro et al., 2016) have emerged as popular tools for making model decisions more transparent. However, as Salih et al. (2024) demonstrate, these methods suffer from significant limitations: their outputs are model-dependent (different classifiers produce different rankings) and are affected by feature collinearity (correlated features receive artificially distributed importance).

This paper replicates the main findings of Salih et al. (2024) using their airline passenger satisfaction case study and extends their work with a Correlation-Aware XAI Robustness Framework.

## 2. Related Work

### 2.1 SHAP
SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) is based on Shapley values from game theory, computing each feature's marginal contribution to the prediction (Lundberg & Lee, 2017). It provides both local and global explanations and has model-specific optimizations (TreeSHAP, LinearSHAP, KernelSHAP).

### 2.2 LIME
LIME (Local Interpretable Model-Agnostic Explanations) explains individual predictions by fitting a local linear model around the instance of interest (Ribeiro et al., 2016). It uses perturbation-based sampling and is inherently local.

### 2.3 Feature Collinearity in XAI
Both methods assume feature independence. When features are correlated, SHAP marginalizes over unrealistic feature combinations (Molnar, 2022), and LIME's linear approximation treats correlated features as independent. Aas et al. (2021) proposed conditional SHAP to address this.

### 2.4 NMR and MIP
Salih et al. (2022) introduced NMR to measure ranking stability, and later MIP (Salih et al., 2024b) to adjust rankings for collinearity.

## 3. Research Gap

Despite existing solutions, no unified framework combines collinearity detection, feature grouping, ranking adjustment, and stability quantification. Group-level explanations are rarely provided, and LIME stability across random seeds is not systematically measured. We address this gap.

## 4. Methodology

### 4.1 Dataset
Airline Passenger Satisfaction from Kaggle (103,904 records, 22 features). Following the paper, we sample 500 subjects (250 satisfied) with stratified random sampling and an 80/20 train/test split.

### 4.2 Classifiers
Four models with default parameters: Decision Tree, Logistic Regression, LightGBM, Support Vector Classifier.

### 4.3 SHAP and LIME
SHAP explanations use model-appropriate explainers (TreeSHAP for DT/LGBM, LinearSHAP for LR, KernelSHAP for SVC). LIME explanations use 5000 perturbation samples.

### 4.4 NMR and MIP
Reimplemented from the original descriptions. NMR iteratively removes top features and measures ranking displacement. MIP adjusts rankings based on observed positional shifts.

### 4.5 Proposed Extension
Our Correlation-Aware XAI Robustness Framework:
1. **Feature grouping:** Hierarchical clustering on correlation matrix (threshold=0.6)
2. **Group-level importance:** Aggregate SHAP values within groups
3. **Correlation-aware ranking:** Redistribute importance credit within groups
4. **Multi-seed stability:** Spearman, Kendall, Jaccard metrics across 5+ seeds

## 9. Discussion

### 9.1 Findings
The replication confirms the paper's key finding: XAI explanations are model-dependent and collinearity-affected. Our extension provides complementary information through grouped explanations.

### 9.2 Limitations
- Our 500-subject sample may differ from the paper's (unknown seed)
- NMR/MIP reimplemented from descriptions, not original code
- The correlation threshold (0.6) is a design choice that affects grouping
- Our extension does not claim to "solve" collinearity in SHAP/LIME

## 10. Limitations and Threats to Validity

1. **Sampling:** Cannot exactly reproduce the paper's 500-subject sample
2. **Implementation:** NMR/MIP may differ from original in edge cases
3. **Generalizability:** Results are specific to this dataset
4. **Threshold sensitivity:** Feature grouping depends on correlation threshold
5. **Computational cost:** KernelSHAP for SVC is an approximation

## 11. Conclusion

We successfully replicated the main findings of Salih et al. (2024) regarding model dependency and collinearity effects on SHAP and LIME. Our Correlation-Aware XAI Robustness Framework offers a complementary approach to MIP, providing grouped explanations and stability quantification. The framework is post-hoc and model-agnostic, applicable to any XAI method and classifier.
